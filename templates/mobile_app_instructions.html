Sampling App: a guide for field researchers

How to install the app, record a sample from start to finish, and get your data synced.

The SPUN Sampling app can replace a notebook or metadata recording sheet. The metadata for every individual sample, including GPS position, photos, and field notes, is saved directly into the app on your phone. This works with no signal at all, though you will need to sync the data you input when you reach signal or WiFi after sampling.

Good accuracy matters, so aim for green. If you're under tree cover or indoors, give it three minutes — the badge updates live as your fix improves.

Sometimes the badge just won't turn green no matter how long you wait — that's a real limit of the phone's GPS in that spot, not something you're doing wrong. If it doesn't improve, use an external GPS device if you're carrying one (see Manual GPS entry) rather than waiting indefinitely.

New sample form

Once the accuracy badge looks good, tap the + button. The form opens and starts capturing your GPS position straight away — you'll see it lock in at the top of the screen.

  • ①Sample ID — required. No spaces (the app strips any you type) and it must start with a letter, e.g. AMZ001. Keep it short: long IDs are hard to write legibly on a sample tube, and the app will warn you if yours is getting long.
  • ②Sample type, Sample or control, Soil depth, Grid size, Agricultural land — tap each to pick from a list. After your first sample in a project, these repeat your last answer automatically, so you usually only touch the ones that changed. Date, coordinates, and elevation are filled in for you.
  • ③Photos — Camera takes a new photo on the spot, Gallery attaches one you already took. Add as many as you need; tap the ✕ on a thumbnail to remove one. Take photos that document both the sampling site and the sample itself. Photos are helpful for remembering aspects of the site such as dominant vegetation, vegetation coverage, and soil type.
  • ④Save sample — saves straight to your phone, no signal required. You're returned to the map with a new pin marking the sample.
Unsure what to put in the various fields? Check the instructions for the metadata.
Carrying an external GPS device? Always photograph the screen that displays the GPS coordinates for the sample you are recording, and attach this image under Photos alongside your other sample photos — every time, not just when the phone’s GPS is struggling.

There's also a free-text Vegetation and Notes field further down the form, for anything worth recording that doesn't fit a dropdown.

Editing a saved sample

Tap any sample in the list on the map screen to reopen it with everything you entered, pre-filled. Change whatever needs correcting, add or remove photos, then tap Save changes.

Manual GPS entry (fallback)

If the phone's GPS can't get a fix — dense canopy, indoors, a faulty sensor — or the accuracy just won't improve, switch on Manual coordinates on the sample form and type the reading from an external handheld GPS device instead.

Use decimal degrees with a dot as the separator, in WGS84 — for example 41.23412, not 41,23412. Handheld units often show degrees/minutes — convert to decimal degrees before typing it in.

Syncing your data

The app is offline-first: everything saves to your phone the moment you tap Save. Nothing uploads until you're back on Wi-Fi or mobile data and open the project again.

If anything is waiting to upload, the map screen header shows a button like ↑ 3s+5p (3 samples, 5 photos pending). Tap it to sync. Each sample's card then shows Synced or Pending, so you always know what's made it to the server.

Do a manual sync before you finish for the day rather than assuming it happened in the background, especially on the last day of a field trip.

If SPUN asks you to re-send photos for this project — for example because some appear to be missing on the server — tap the ↺ button. It marks every photo in the project as unsynced and uploads them all again. Only use it if asked to: it re-uploads everything, which takes time and data.

Exporting data & map layers

↓ CSV exports the samples you've recorded in this project as a spreadsheet-ready file you can share or archive.

+ Layer imports a .gpkg (GeoPackage) file — plot boundaries or a sampling grid, for example — as a reference overlay on your map. Long-press a layer's chip below the header to remove it.

Troubleshooting

  • Badge stuck on "Acquiring GPS…" — give it a few seconds outdoors, and check the app has Location permission in your phone's Settings.
  • Badge stays orange or red — move to open sky, away from tall buildings or dense canopy, and wait a minute. If it genuinely won't improve, that's a limit of the phone's GPS at that spot — switch to an external GPS device and manual coordinates instead (see Manual GPS entry).
  • Camera or Gallery doesn't respond — allow the permission prompt the first time you tap it; if you already denied it, re-enable it for the app in your phone's Settings.
  • Can't sign in — check your internet connection first. If it keeps failing, contact {{ support_email }}.
